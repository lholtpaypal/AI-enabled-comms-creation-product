The opportunity

The ideas aren't the bottleneck. Building them is.

Push notifications are one of our most powerful levers, yet in a typical week more than half of customers hear nothing from us. Until now, one campaign meant roughly 58 hours of work across 11 APIs — near-impossible to run at any volume. The fix: an agent-based workflow that makes creation cheap enough to close the gap. Our best growth channel is about to be supercharged.

Why it's hard

Creating one push means gathering context from across the company.

Customers don't tell us they're out of ideas — they tell us the build is the hard part.

1

The context to build a campaign is scattered across the enterprise — more teams, more handoffs to pull it together.

2

Every step is the same motion: a fresh person retrieves context and enters it — copy, audience, deep link, experiment. The shape repeats.

3

Teaching a person is bespoke onboarding, repeated for every person, team, and campaign. It never compounds.

4

The result: only about 3% of ideas make it through — and the ones that ship often lose their personalization on the way.

The point

Most of this work is moving data between systems — retrieving context, translating it, filling fields — which is exactly what agents are good at. That groundwork proves the path; agents take it the rest of the way.

The workflow

An agent for each job a person does today.

Set context up once and every team gets it — no re-onboarding, no scavenger hunt.

Five agents, each doing a specialist's job — set up once, not re-trained for every team. That's cost that piles up versus capacity that compounds.

What it gets us

We think we'll be overflowing with comms in three months.

The context lives in the agents now — built once, reused for every campaign — so volume no longer depends on headcount. What took ~58 hours across 11 APIs now takes ~3.5 hours across 3.

Where this goes

Saturation is a good problem — and the next thing to plan for.

Going from too few comms to almost too many is the goal. But at volume, the hard question changes from “can we make it?” to “which message, to whom, when?” — a problem intelligence solves, not headcount. Here's how we get there:

  1. Set guardrails and benchmarks per agent. Measure each before we trust it — nothing ships ahead of the data.

  2. Right-size the model. Start with the most capable, work back to the cheapest that clears the bar.

  3. Keep a person in the loop through launch. People own strategy and edge cases; agents do the data movement.

  4. Flip from pull to push. Use data to find under-served markets, build the package, and bring it for one-click approve or edit.

Where we'll need help

Resourcing the intelligence layer.

The workflow gets us to volume fast; staying ahead of it — what to send, to whom, and when — is what the intelligence layer is for, and the next thing we'll need to staff.
